# Oral-and-Pancreatic-Cancer-Biomarker-Panel-Discovery-with-Machine-Learning-and-Clinical-Validation
Biomarker Panel Discovery
Oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC) and pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) are highly lethal malignancies presenting substantial global health burden primarily due to lacking reliable screening and their aggressive progression. Current diagnostic modalities rely heavily on invasive tissue biopsies and imaging, which frequently prove ineffective for early-stage cancer detection. To address this diagnostic gap, we present a novel, non-invasive liquid biopsy platform that synergizes computational analytics with an ultrasensitive Janus particle-based immunoassay. Machine Learning was utilized initially to determine potential exosomal surface biomarkers from open-source online LC-MS databases, subsequently validated for OSCC and PDAC prediction accuracy. We fabricated anisotropic, half-metal-coated Janus particles functionalized with targeting antibodies to rapidly quantify low-abundance surface markers with a panel including CD9, CDH3, TACSTD2, and ITGA3 directly from complex biological samples. Upon binding target exosomes, the rotational Brownian motion of the Janus particles is dynamically hindered. This physical alteration is precisely measured as a decrease in rotational fluorescence blinking frequency via deep learning-based particle tracking algorithm. The immunoassay's performance was rigorously evaluated utilizing characterized OSCC (CAL-27, HSC-3, SAS) and PDAC (AsPC-1, BxPC-3, PANC-1) cell lines, alongside healthy oral keratinocytes (hNOK). For clinical validation, patient plasma samples were collected, purified by TIM-4 isolation, and analyzed using the Janus particle-based immunoassay. Furthermore, we employed machine learning classification models to systematically evaluate optimal biomarker combinations. Frequency spectrum analysis demonstrated a statistically significant decrease in blinking frequency for cancer samples compared to healthy controls. Crucially, our model validation revealed that a focused four-marker panel outperformed all singular biomarkers in classification accuracy. Notably, the platform demonstrated exceptional accuracy of 92.6%, establishing a rigorous limit of detection of 10 7 exosomes/ml capable of quantifying minimal exosomal concentrations. By integrating AI-driven biomarker discovery with anisotropic microparticle diffusometry, this scalable platform achieves superior sensitivity, offering profound translational potential for precision oncology and multi-cancer screening.
